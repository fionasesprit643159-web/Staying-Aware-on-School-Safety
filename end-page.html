Classroom checklist

Review these steps as part of your school’s safety plan.

1Did you lock the door?
2Did you turn off the lights?
3Did you barricade the door?
4Did you turn off the projector/TV?
5Did you hide in the cabinet?
Remember

In the classroom, always remember to lock the doors, turn off any lights, barricade the door with furniture, and stay hidden. Have an escape route and plan in mind, and be prepared to evacuate regardless of whether others agree to follow. Leave your belongings behind, as they may slow you down. If faced with the shooter, be prepared to physically fight for your life. Work together with your peers nearby to safely survive. Once you are safe, call 911.

Bathroom checklist

Review these steps as part of your school’s safety plan.

1Did you lock the door?
2Did you barricade the door?
3Did you hide in an inner stall?
4Did you lock the stall door?
5Are you hidden from view?
6Did you maintain silence?
7Are you prepared to fight only as a last resort?
Remember

It is essential to hide in the nearest shelter at school if there is a shooter. If stuck in the bathroom, always remember to lock the doors if there is a lock. Hide and lock yourself in the inner stall, make yourself hidden from view. If there is furniture in the bathroom, use it to barricade the door. Stand on top of the toilet to remain hidden, and remain silent. If faced with the shooter, be prepared to physically fight for your life. Work together with your peers nearby to safely survive. Once you are safe, call 911.

Outdoor checklist

Review these steps as part of your school’s safety plan.

1Did you leave your belongings behind?
2Did you use a safe exit away from the threat?
3If you could not safely exit, did you hide in a secure place out of sight?
4Did you lock or block the entry?
5If met with the shooter, did you fight?
Remember

If you are unable to evacuate to a safe space at school, it’s vital to know how to stay safe in open spaces outdoors. If stuck outside in the quads or hallways, remember to leave your belongings behind if it slows you down, and barricade any entries to the school with nearby trashcans or tables. Try to evacuate from the school and use a safe exit away from the threat. If unable to escape, hide under tables and in trees. If faced with the shooter, be prepared to physically fight for your life. Work together with your peers nearby to safely survive. Once you are safe, call 911.

Why awareness and support matter

Exposure to gun violence and the fear of violence can affect students’ wellbeing, sense of safety, and learning. Sandy Hook Promise emphasizes prevention through noticing when someone may be in crisis, taking concerns seriously, and connecting them with help early.

1
Notice changes and warning signsReach out if a student seems isolated, distressed, or says something that raises concern.
2
Tell a trusted adultShare concerns with a teacher, counselor, family member, or another trusted adult. Do not handle a serious concern alone.
3
Build a supportive school communityListening without judgment and helping students find support can make it easier to ask for help.

Sandy Hook Promise reports that students who received its prevention training were better able to recognize warning signs, take them seriously, and get help. Read its research findings or learn about the Say Something program.

Information to provide to law enforcement or 911 operator

  • Location of the active shooter
  • Number of shooters, if more than one
  • Physical description of shooter/s
  • Number and type of weapons held by the shooter/s
  • Number of potential victims at the location
